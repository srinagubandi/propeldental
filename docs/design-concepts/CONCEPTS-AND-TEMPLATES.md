# Propel Dental — Design Concepts and Expansion Templates

**Phase:** Visual concept exploration only. Every example metric, quotation, portrait, practice name, and long-form text in the renders is intentionally illustrative. Screen body copy uses Latin placeholder text until approved content is supplied.

## Shared operating rules

Each direction keeps the current Propel Dental logo and core offer in view, speaks first to a dental-practice owner or decision-maker, and provides an explicit **Get in touch** action at every major decision point. Operations leaders can continue into a method, proof, or service route. The concepts preserve the existing public route set as described in the retention plan and add the proposed templates only after approval.[1]

| Shared requirement | Treatment in all concepts |
|---|---|
| Primary conversion | `Get in touch` stays visually dominant, with a plain-language explanation of the next step in production. |
| Brand continuity | Existing logo retained. Orange remains the decisive emphasis/interaction signal. |
| Placeholder policy | New body content uses Latin text. Placeholder visuals carry an `Illustrative content` label. |
| Case-study proof | A case study must show evidence type, source/owner, review date, scope, and limitation before a quantitative outcome or testimonial is published. |
| Team profiles | Portraits, names, titles, biographies, and links wait for owner-provided approval and rights. |
| Static first | Every screen works without video, scroll animation, hover, or motion. |
| Responsive behavior | At narrow widths, hierarchy and reading order change; desktop grids do not simply shrink. |

## Concept A — **The Evidence Ledger**

This direction makes the decision process feel tangible and accountable. It treats a practice owner as a careful buyer who wants to understand what will happen, who will own each step, and where proof is documented. It is the most system-oriented direction.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| Premise | Propel is the clear, accountable growth partner for a consequential full-arch program decision. |
| Desktop composition | An asymmetric 7/5 split hero. A strong text and contact column sits beside a vertical evidence ledger that previews the P90 stages, a case-study card, and proof-label anatomy. |
| Mobile composition | The contact action appears directly after the opening statement. The ledger becomes a single vertical sequence of large, numbered decision panels. No sidebar or dense dashboard is retained. |
| Proof container | A structured ledger: `Context`, `Method`, `Evidence`, `Scope`. Case cards expose evidence status before any outcome. |
| Art direction | Near-black graphite, warm white, current signal orange, hairline rules, paper-like metadata bands, abstract implant geometry. No literal patient imagery. |
| Type and spacing | Condensed/authoritative display role with a practical sans-serif interface role. Tight editorial alignment, 8-point spacing rhythm, low-radius panels. |
| CTA framing | `Get in touch` with a small next-step statement such as `Responsum intra unum diem utilem` in the final design after wording approval. |
| Best fit | A buyer prioritizing transparency, operating rigor, and visible process. |

### Concept A token sheet

| Token | Value / behavior |
|---|---|
| Canvas | `#0B1014` graphite with `#F4F0E8` document surfaces. |
| Signal | Orange approximating the live brand accent, reserved for action, stage state, and focal words. |
| Display role | Large high-contrast grotesk; one dominant message per viewport. |
| Components | Evidence ledger, stage marker, proof label, case-study module, route tag, contact rail. |
| Image treatment | Abstract, desaturated office/process stills; a thin orange crop edge identifies editorial media. |

### Concept A page sequence

1. Positioning and `Get in touch`.
2. P90 operating ledger.
3. Selected case-study evidence modules.
4. Capabilities organized by decision objective.
5. Team directory as named roles and practice disciplines.
6. Closing contact rail with phone fallback.

## Concept B — **The Practice Story**

This direction makes the people and context behind the work the persuasive center. It replaces a technical, grid-led first impression with a calm editorial story that starts with a human team image, then moves through selected work, the P90 method, and the people who deliver it. It is the most editorial and relationship-forward direction.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| Premise | The strongest differentiator is an involved partner that learns a practice before proposing a plan. |
| Desktop composition | A full-bleed editorial spread. The hero uses a large original, rights-cleared team or practice-environment image with an anchored text block and a minimal navigation line. Case studies enter as oversized image-and-caption chapters. |
| Mobile composition | A story-first sequence: opening image, concise proposition, immediate contact action, then an alternating vertical rhythm of method, featured work, and people. Captions move below images and no text sits on unpredictable photography. |
| Proof container | A narrative case chapter: context, turning point, method, and evidence disclosure. One verified result may appear beside the relevant step only after approval. |
| Art direction | Warm bone paper, deep navy ink, current orange in restrained strokes, tactile grain, editorial photography, large intentional whitespace. |
| Type and spacing | Expressive serif display role plus neutral sans-serif UI role. Generous reading measure and 16/24/48 spacing cadence. |
| CTA framing | `Get in touch` follows the first story beat and appears again after selected work. The final implementation should explain response expectation and general-contact alternative. |
| Best fit | A buyer who wants to understand the people, philosophy, and selectivity behind a partnership. |

### Concept B token sheet

| Token | Value / behavior |
|---|---|
| Canvas | `#F5F0E8` warm paper; text in `#101923`; charcoal editorial blocks. |
| Signal | Current brand orange used as an underlining stroke, index number, and contact button. |
| Display role | Characterful but legible editorial serif; never used for dense navigation or metadata. |
| Components | Image chapter, pull quote placeholder, thin-caption rail, person card, selected-work tile, contact panel. |
| Image treatment | Original team/process/practice photography with visible `Illustrative content` placeholder state until assets are approved. |

### Concept B page sequence

1. Human context and `Get in touch`.
2. A short reason-for-being story.
3. P90 as a three-chapter visual sequence.
4. Selected case-study cards that lead to detailed narratives.
5. Team portraits and brief role introductions.
6. A low-pressure final contact chapter.

## Concept C — **The Growth Atlas**

This direction presents Propel as a strategic navigator. It uses a layered, map-like system to show that full-arch growth involves connected disciplines rather than isolated channels. It is the most visual and modular direction, without implying that a prototype visualization represents live client data.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| Premise | A full-arch growth program is a connected system of market context, demand generation, conversion readiness, and measurement. |
| Desktop composition | A large navigable atlas-style hero: a central proposition surrounded by four objective zones. The first scroll transitions into service pathways, case-study routes, and a visible team capability map. |
| Mobile composition | The atlas becomes a guided sequence of four labeled panels. The primary action remains above the first panel; each subsequent section has one clear route rather than a miniature map. |
| Proof container | A linked pathway from `Question` to `Method` to `Evidence`. Case-study cards show the relevant service path and an illustrative evidence label. |
| Art direction | Deep ink, warm white surfaces, orange signal nodes, muted mineral blue-green, soft directional lines, restrained geometry. |
| Type and spacing | Contemporary sans-serif display and interface pairing, highly legible labels, modular 12-column desktop grid, roomy mobile panels. |
| CTA framing | `Get in touch` is positioned as the first step in mapping the practice context. A secondary `Explore the method` link supports operations leaders. |
| Best fit | A buyer who wants to see how channels, operations, and measurement connect before starting a conversation. |

### Concept C token sheet

| Token | Value / behavior |
|---|---|
| Canvas | `#10151C` ink, `#EFF1EC` light canvas, and muted `#A9C1BE` information surface. |
| Signal | Orange node and action state; never used to represent an actual performance status. |
| Display role | Geometric sans-serif with large spacing; labels use sentence case for clarity. |
| Components | Atlas node, path card, service zone, proof route, team capability cluster, contact waypoint. |
| Image treatment | Original abstract diagrams, texture, and approved process photography; no fabricated dashboards, charts, or client-data simulations. |

### Concept C page sequence

1. Growth atlas opening and `Get in touch`.
2. Four connected growth zones.
3. P90 as an implementation path.
4. Case-study index organized by the buyer’s question or service path.
5. Team capability clusters with links to profiles.
6. Contact waypoint and telephone fallback.

## Demonstrated divergence

The directions differ on more than four dimensions, including multiple structural dimensions.

| Dimension | Concept A — Evidence Ledger | Concept B — Practice Story | Concept C — Growth Atlas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero / page composition | Split dossier and proof rail | Full-bleed editorial spread | Central atlas with connected zones |
| Information / proof container | Evidence ledger | Narrative chapter | Linked method/evidence pathway |
| Primary conversion framing | Accountable inquiry | Partnership conversation | Context-mapping conversation |
| Mobile composition | Decision panels | Story chapters | Guided pathway panels |
| Art direction | Technical document | Warm editorial image story | Strategic mapping system |
| Type / density | Compact and structured | Generous and expressive | Modular and spatial |
| Case-study browsing | Ledger cards | Image chapters | Pathway-indexed cards |
| Team expression | Role directory | Portrait-led people section | Capability clusters |

## Case-study templates

### Index template: `/case-studies`

The index contains a short, non-claiming introduction, an optional filter set that is usable without JavaScript, a grid of approved stories, and a closing contact panel. A card should never display a metric, quote, practice identity, or recognizable photograph without its approved evidence record.

| Component | Required fields | Concept-phase placeholder |
|---|---|---|
| Index introduction | Heading, contextual summary, source/approval note. | `Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet, consectetur adipiscing elit.` |
| Filter | Practice type, service path, market context; optional and keyboard accessible. | `Omnis`, `Strategia`, `Creatio`, `Mensura`. |
| Case card | Practice name/permission state, descriptor, approved visual, short challenge, evidence label, route. | `Praxis Illustrativa`, Latin text, abstract image, `Illustrative content`. |
| Evidence label | Evidence type, source/owner, review date, scope/limitation. | `Evidentia illustrativa — recensio pendens`. |
| CTA | Contact route and expected next step. | `Get in touch`. |

### Detail template: `/case-studies/[slug]`

| Section | Purpose | Production requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Case header | Establish the practice context without exposing patient information. | Approved name, location granularity, service context, permissions. |
| Scope label | States what the story demonstrates and what it does not. | Approved evidence type, owner, review date, limitations. |
| Challenge | Describe the business/operational context. | Must not imply clinical outcomes. |
| Method | Explain relevant strategy, creative, team/process, and measurement work. | Substantiate described methods; no confidential client data. |
| Evidence modules | Present the smallest useful set of verified facts. | Methodology, timeframe, source, approval, and contextual limitation for each statement. |
| Approved perspective | Optional testimonial or quote. | Written permission, named attribution policy, review date. |
| Related path | Link to the relevant service or P90 stage. | Preserve existing internal routes. |
| Contact | Clear `Get in touch` action and telephone alternative. | State expected follow-up and do not request patient information. |

## Team templates

### Team directory: `/about#team`

The directory introduces the team as a set of approved roles and disciplines, not as a collection of invented credentials. The desktop arrangement may be a portrait grid, role directory, or capability cluster depending on the selected concept. Mobile presents one full-width person card at a time with a clear tap target.

| Component | Required fields | Placeholder treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Portrait | Rights-cleared headshot and descriptive alt text. | Neutral, non-identifying placeholder portrait marked `Illustrative content`. |
| Identity | Approved name and title. | `Persona Prima` and `Officium Illustrativum`. |
| Short bio | Approved one- or two-sentence biography. | Latin text only. |
| Discipline tags | Approved areas of expertise. | `Strategia`, `Creatio`, `Mensura`. |
| Profile route | Optional detailed bio page. | `/team/[slug]` only after content/SEO approval. |

### Team profile: `/team/[slug]`

| Section | Purpose | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Profile header | Portrait, approved name, role, and concise bio. | Avoid credentials or claims that have not been verified. |
| Practice perspective | Explains the person’s relevant role in the engagement. | Keep business expertise separate from clinical advice. |
| Focus areas | Up to four specific service/process strengths. | Use approved language. |
| Selected contributions | Optional links to approved non-confidential work. | Do not expose client data or patient information. |
| Contact | Routes to the general contact action. | No direct personal contact without consent. |

## Asset and handoff checklist

Before an implementation phase, the owner should supply a current logo package, approved team headshots, biographies, selected case-study permissions, evidence sources, methodology notes, review dates, and any approved practice imagery. The implementation phase should then update the claims/proof log, literal-copy lockfile, redirect plan, and viewport QA record; it should not treat these visual concepts as approval for changing live content.
